Our Approach

Kids learn better from someone who was just in their shoes.

Most students meet entrepreneurship for the first time in a college elective, if they meet it at all. By then the habits that matter most, spotting a real problem, testing an idea cheaply, treating money like it runs out, have already been shaped by someone else's curriculum. Genesis starts years earlier, and hands the teaching to people who still remember what it's like to sit in that classroom.

01

Peer-taught, from the ground up

Every camp and workshop at Genesis is designed and led by teens, not adults guessing what teenagers want to hear. The instructor at the front of the room was a middle schooler a few years ago too, which changes what questions students are willing to ask.

02

Entrepreneurship and money, together

Genesis pairs hands-on entrepreneurship with financial literacy: budgeting, saving, and the basics of running something, in the same program. Neither half makes much sense without the other, so they're never split into separate units.

03

Started by a national finalist
