Our Approach

“Guiding Emerging Next-Gen Entrepreneurial Success through inclusive programs designed by youth, for youth.”

Built around one idea: students learn best from people close to their own age.

Most students meet entrepreneurship for the first time in a college elective, if they meet it at all. By then, the habits that actually matter, noticing a real problem, testing a rough idea cheaply, handling money like it's finite, have usually already been shaped by someone else's curriculum. Genesis starts that conversation years earlier, and hands the teaching to people who remember exactly what it's like to sit in that classroom.

01

Peer-taught, from the ground up

Every camp and workshop at Genesis is designed and led by teens, not adults guessing what teenagers want to hear. The instructor at the front of the room was a middle schooler a few years ago too, which changes what questions students are willing to ask.

02

Entrepreneurship and money, together

Genesis pairs hands-on entrepreneurship with financial literacy: budgeting, saving, and the basics of running something, in the same program. Neither half makes much sense without the other, so they're never split into separate units.

03

Started by a national finalist

Co-founder Siddharth Kommuri placed 8th nationally and 3rd regionally out of 650+ teams in a virtual entrepreneurship simulation. Genesis is the program he built so more students could have that shot sooner, and earlier than a national competition most schools never mention.
